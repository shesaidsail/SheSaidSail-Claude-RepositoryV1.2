# She Said Sail — Conversion Strategy
**Version:** 1.0
**Branch:** feature/luxury-conversion-overhaul

---

## CONVERSION PHILOSOPHY

She Said Sail is not converting a commodity purchase.
It is converting a $10,000+ group celebration decision.

The buyer is:
- The person organizing for others (high emotional responsibility)
- Researching on behalf of a group (social risk)
- Deciding between multiple options (comparison phase)
- Uncertain about logistics (anxiety)

The site's job is to remove every source of anxiety before it surfaces.
Not by over-explaining. By signaling: this is handled, this is for you, others have trusted this.

---

## PRIMARY CONVERSION ACTION

One action: Request to Book.

Not: browse, explore, plan, discover.
One ask. One path. One button.

All CTAs should lead to `/request-to-book/` once a visitor has seen the product.

The experiences page `/experiences/` is a valid middle-funnel step, but the homepage CTA hierarchy should prioritize booking intent over browsing intent.

## CONVERSION FUNNEL MAP

```
Homepage hero
  Visitor sees photography, headline, occasion pills
  Feels: "This is for me."
  Action: Scroll or click Request to Book

Experience cards
  Visitor sees 4 named experiences
  Feels: "There is one that fits my group."
  Action: Click "Explore Experience" into a specific experience page

Social proof strip
  Visitor reads 3 emotional quotes
  Feels: "Others have done this. It was worth it."
  Action: Continue scrolling toward commitment

"Not Just a Charter" section
  Visitor reads what is included
  Feels: "I understand what I am paying for."
  Action: Click "Plan Your Experience" or "Request to Book"

Email capture (for non-ready visitors)
  Visitor is interested but not yet ready
  Feels: "I can stay connected without committing."
  Action: Enter email, enter nurture sequence

Bottom CTA banner
  Final page conversion prompt
  Feels: "Now I am ready."
  Action: Click "Request to Book"
```

## TRUST STACK SEQUENCE

A luxury visitor needs to feel trust before committing to an inquiry.
The trust stack on the homepage should build in this order:

1. Photography — "This looks real and beautiful."
2. Named experiences — "There is structure here. This is not improvised."
3. Social proof — "People like me have done this."
4. Service description — "I understand what I am getting."
5. Price anchor — "I can afford this or I cannot. Either way, I know now."
6. CTA — "I am ready to ask."

Every element should reinforce, not interrupt, this sequence.

---

## EMAIL NURTURE STRATEGY

Visitors who give their email are expressing interest but not yet booking intent.
Treat them as warm leads, not subscribers.

Recommended nurture sequence (3 emails, 7-day spread):
1. Day 0: "Here is what a full day looks like" (visual content, experience overview)
2. Day 4: "The details most people ask about" (FAQ-style, logistics, what is included)
3. Day 7: "When you are ready, we are here" (soft invitation to book, no urgency)

Subject lines should feel observational, not promotional.
Voice: warm, composed, one person writing to another.

---

## SEASONALITY AND URGENCY

The brand governance prohibits fake scarcity.
Real urgency exists naturally: Miami charter availability is limited, especially in season.

Approved real-urgency copy (seasonal, not fake):
- "Summer dates book 6 to 8 weeks ahead."
- "October through April books quickly."

These are factual. Use them in the nurture sequence and on the booking request form, not as homepage countdown timers.

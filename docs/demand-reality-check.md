# Demand Reality Check: Will the Market Pay the Cost-Up Prices

The portfolio strategy set cost-up target prices. This checks them against actual 2026 Miami competitor listings before any change. The headline: cost-up pricing is correct in principle but hits a hard market ceiling on two prestige yachts, where the Gale cost already exceeds the market retail for comparable vessels. No production pricing changed.

## Competitor pricing anchors (2026 Miami, real listings)

- Pershing 80, Miami, 4 hours from $6,420 plus 15 percent gratuity (Billionaires Miami, bluebnc).
- Large luxury 65 to 85ft: about $5,000 to $12,000 per half day. 70 to 100ft: about $8,000 to $14,000 per full day (Miami Yachting Company, Miami Living 2026 report).
- 90ft Sunseeker class: roughly $700 per hour, so about $2,800 to $4,200 for a half day on entry listings, up to $5,000 to $9,000 for premium 90 to 95ft.
- Most competitor rates include captain, crew, and fuel, but food, decor, and gratuity are extra and add about 30 to 50 percent.

To compare like for like, She Said Sail is all inclusive and produced, so the fair benchmark is competitor charter plus about 40 percent for the food, decor, and service a client would otherwise add. That gives an all in market ceiling per class.

## The four you asked about

### Sugaree at $24,000
Comparable 72ft Bluegame or similar charters for about $5,500 to $7,000 for 4 hours, all in maybe $8,000 to $10,500. Sugaree is already above the market ceiling at its current $15,500. At $24,000 it is roughly two to three times the comparable all in price. It would not convert. Worse, your cost is $11,250, which is itself near or above the market retail for the class, so even at a market price you make little. Verdict: do not raise to $24,000. This is a cost problem, not a price problem.

### GTX 80 at $26,500
A Pershing 80 charters in Miami from $6,420 for 4 hours. Your Gale cost is $12,600, about double the market retail for the same boat. At $26,500 you would be roughly four times the market. It would not convert. The vessel is structurally unprofitable at any market clearing price, because the input cost exceeds what the market pays. Verdict: do not raise. Renegotiate the Gale rate, feature it only for buyers who specifically want a brand new Pershing, or drop it.

### Gatsby at $19,900
Comparable 90 to 100ft yachts charter for about $8,000 to $12,000, all in about $13,000 to $17,000 for a produced experience. Your cost is $9,900. $19,900 sits just above the top of the ceiling. It can convert for a fully produced, concierge led luxury day, but it is at the edge. Recommended: increase, but to about $18,500, not $19,900, to stay inside the believable range.

### Mirracle at $16,500
Comparable 90 to 95ft Sunseeker all in about $10,000 to $14,000. Your cost is $7,650. $16,500 is at the top of the ceiling. It can convert as a premium all inclusive option, but expect some softening. Recommended: about $15,000 to $15,500 is safer, $16,500 is the maximum.

Flagships are different. True 105 to 112ft full day produced charters compare to $14,000 to $25,000 plus extras, so an all in $38,000 to $46,000 is defensible to high net worth buyers at low volume. The flagship increases hold. The mid prestige boats (Sugaree, GTX 80) do not.

## What this means

1. The founder instinct is correct. Volume fleet leave alone, Core fleet moderate increases, Prestige validate before jumping. The cost-up math overshot on Sugaree and GTX 80.
2. Demand does change after repricing. At the cost-up prices, Sugaree and GTX 80 would lose most demand because they sit far above the comparable all in market price.
3. Sugaree and GTX 80 are cost problems, not pricing problems. Their Gale input cost is near or above the market retail for their class. The fix is to renegotiate the Gale rate, use them only on request, or retire them, not to raise the price into a vacuum.
4. Core increases are real but capped by the ceiling. Mirracle to about $15,000 and Gatsby to about $18,500 are sustainable. Pushing to the full cost-up $16,500 and $19,900 risks conversion.
5. The Volume fleet is the safe growth engine. It is comfortably inside the market ceiling and should carry penetration and review volume.

## Revised guidance versus the cost-up model

- Keep Volume as is.
- Raise Core moderately (Mirracle to $15,000, Gatsby to $18,500), not to the full cost-up number.
- Do not raise Sugaree and GTX 80 to cost-up. Hold near current, renegotiate Gale, or limit to on request. Treat their thin or negative margin as the signal to fix the supply cost.
- Hold the flagship increases (Another One, Carpe Diem Premium), where the luxury market supports them at low volume.

## Still needed

- Compass Gale rate, to place it.
- Confirmed F&B and decor cost per tier, to firm the ceilings.
- A target Gale rate renegotiation for Sugaree and GTX 80, since their economics depend on lowering the input cost rather than raising price.

# She Said Sail: Final Operating Model

The one model. Built on real data (Gale costs, Airtable, website, competitor research). Gift bags are an add-on, not a default cost, included only on Monaco Social. Economics below reflect that. No production data changed; this is the approved-on-sign-off operating model.

## Confirmed cost rule change

Gift bags ($100 per guest, about $1,200 at 12) are removed from every booking's cost. They are an upsell. Monaco Social includes them, so only Monaco carries the $1,200. This lifts Volume and Core contribution by about $1,200 per booking versus the earlier gift-on-all assumption.

Standard cost stack per booking (no gift): Boat Cost (Gale x 0.90) + $550 concierge labor + F&B and decor by tier + $150 transportation credit + 5.5% card and commission. Monaco adds $1,200 gift.

## 3. Final package strategy

Keep the four experiences and the existing premiums, they self-select cleanly and the steps are high margin:
- Rosé Day Club: base, 1.00
- Golden Hour Escape: about plus 6%
- Pink Palm Club: about plus 12%
- Monaco Social: about plus 22%, includes gift bags

## 4. Final upsell structure (the margin engine)

Clean all-inclusive base price, luxuries as upsells that drop almost entirely to contribution:
- Gift bags: $195 per guest add-on (included only on Monaco).
- Premium champagne (Veuve) and caviar: add-on, included on Monaco and Pink Palm.
- DJ, photographer, private chef, live music, elevated decor: add-ons (already catalogued).
- Target a 40%+ add-on attach rate. On a $14,000 booking, two add-ons at about $2,250 each add roughly $4,000 of near-pure margin, often more than the base contribution.

## 5. Volume fleet expansion (65 to 75ft)

The best economics in the data are the smaller, cheaper boats, and they still seat 12 for a day charter, which is the bachelorette and birthday core.

- Ideal charter source cost: $4,500 to $6,000 per 4 hours. Never source above the comparable market retail.
- If owning rather than chartering: acquisition roughly $1.5M to $3.5M for a used 65 to 75ft motor yacht, which only makes sense at high utilization; the broker-sourced charter model is lower risk to start.
- Ideal customer-facing price: $9,500 to $12,500 at 4hr, 20 to 25% margin.

Best class by job:
- Best for volume: 65 to 75ft motor yachts (Princess, Azimut, Prestige, older Sunseeker). Cheap to source, 12 guests, easy yes.
- Best for profit: 90 to 96ft (San Lorenzo, Sunseeker). Markup holds and price reaches $15,000 to $19,000.
- Best for prestige: 105 to 112ft (Azimut, San Lorenzo) flagships at $38,000 to $46,000.

## 6. Final homepage strategy

The homepage sells the easiest yachts to book, not the biggest. Feature:
- Hero and primary cards: the Volume fleet, Carpe Diem, Freedom, Vasiliki, Tranquility IV, with a clear from $9,900 entry.
- One aspirational flagship (Another One) as a halo image, not the default choice.
- Do not lead with Sugaree or GTX 80. They are not economic and not the easy yes.

## 7. Final paid ads and email strategy

- Paid ads: Volume fleet only. Lowest entry price, best conversion, best CAC payback. Lead creative with from $9,500 and the easy, all-inclusive celebration.
- Email marketing: Core fleet and upgrades. Nurture existing leads toward Mirracle and Gatsby, the Pink Palm and Monaco tiers, and the add-ons. This is where average booking value grows.
- Prestige only: GTX 80 (until renegotiated) and the two flagships are concierge and inquiry only, never paid ad entry points.

## 8. Final supplier strategy

- Keep the six well-priced Gale boats.
- Renegotiate Sugaree and GTX 80 toward roughly half their current Gale rate, or replace with better-sourced 72 to 80ft vessels.
- Diversify sourcing by adding 65 to 75ft value boats, ideally from more than one broker.
- Hard rule: never accept a source cost above the comparable market retail for that class.

## Expected economics (gift-free standard, Monaco carries gift)

Blended contribution per booking: Volume about $3,200, Core about $6,000, Prestige about $13,000.

| Bookings/month | Contribution | Less $10,000 overhead | Monthly profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | about $145,000 | minus $10,000 | about $135,000 |
| 50 | about $271,000 | minus $10,000 | about $261,000 |
| 100 | about $545,000 | minus $10,000 | about $535,000 |

Prestige counts are a demand ceiling, not a guarantee. Volume and Core alone clear overhead many times over. Break-even is about 2 to 3 bookings per month.

## The three levers

- Single biggest profit lever: average booking value, through the add-on attach rate and steering to higher experience tiers. Add-ons are near-pure margin and lift every booking with no demand loss, so a 40%+ attach rate adds more profit than any base price change.
- Single biggest growth lever: the competitively priced Volume fleet plus paid ads, feeding the review and referral flywheel. The cheap-to-source boats are the engine for bookings, reviews, and market share.
- Single biggest operational risk: single-supplier dependency. Every yacht currently sources from one broker (Gale, contact Mike Felder). A rate increase or inventory pull breaks the whole model. Diversify sourcing and lock rates as the top operational priority. Secondary risk: availability and double-booking at scale, which the Yacht_Availability holds and the Make automations must enforce.

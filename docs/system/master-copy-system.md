## COPY PHILOSOPHY

Copy does not sell. Copy confirms what the guest already wants to feel.

The page should read like a warm, composed friend who happens to know exactly how to host the perfect day on the water. Not a booking engine. Not a brochure.

---

## VOICE ATTRIBUTES

| Attribute | What it Sounds Like |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Warm | Personal, first-person, attentive |
| Composed | Measured pace, no urgency pressure |
| Observational | Scene-setting, specific details |
| Confident | Assertive without explaining everything |
| Understated | Prestige implied, never announced |

---

## SENTENCE RULES

1. Short sentences preferred. Average 12 words.
2. No em dashes. Ever.
3. No hype language.
4. No "unforgettable", "epic", "amazing", "luxury lifestyle", "elite", "VIP".
5. No fake scarcity ("only a few dates left").
6. No hard close language ("book now before it's too late").
7. No overexplaining. Trust the guest to understand.
8. No rhetorical questions as CTAs ("Ready to sail?").
9. Active voice always.
10. Lead with the feeling, follow with the fact.

## PREFERRED PATTERNS

### Eyebrow text
Uppercase, short, factual.
Examples: "MIAMI · FORT LAUDERDALE" / "PRIVATE CHARTERS" / "PINK PALM CLUB"

### Headlines
Observational and specific. Not generic celebration language.
Good: "Twelve guests. One afternoon. The kind of day they talk about for months."
Bad: "Create unforgettable memories with your closest friends."

### Body copy
Confident and scene-setting.
Good: "Everything is handled before you arrive. You show up, step aboard, and the rest is already in motion."
Bad: "We provide a comprehensive luxury experience tailored to your needs."

### CTA text
Declarative and simple.
Good: "Check availability" / "Request this experience" / "Get the details"
Bad: "Book your dream charter now!" / "Click here to get started"

---

## EMOTIONAL ARC PER PAGE

Each experience page should move the reader through:

1. Recognition (this is for me)
2. Curiosity (what does this include)
3. Desire (I can picture myself there)
4. Trust (these people know what they're doing)
5. Action (I want to inquire)

Copy should not rush from step 1 to step 5. Let the page breathe.

---

## SECTION-LEVEL COPY STANDARDS

### Hero
- Eyebrow: product/occasion label
- Headline: scene-based, emotionally specific, 10 words max
- Subhead: one supporting sentence, conversational
- CTA: single, declarative action

### Experience Description
- Lead with the emotional promise
- Follow with the operational certainty
- 2 to 3 short paragraphs maximum
- No bullet-point laundry lists in this section

### What is Included
- Bullet format acceptable
- Keep each point to one short line
- Lead with the noun (captain, food, music) not the adjective

### Pricing / Booking
- Price stated clearly. Never hidden.
- Reassurance language adjacent to price (not salesy, just certain)
- CTA immediately after pricing

### Social Proof
- Real language. Not templated.
- Location and occasion context where possible
- Maximum 3 quotes on a page

### Footer CTA Section
- One headline
- One sentence support
- One CTA
- No duplication of hero language

---

## REASSURANCE LANGUAGE PATTERNS

Use these patterns to reduce anxiety without sounding desperate:

- "You'll hear from us within one business day."
- "We handle every detail. You just confirm."
- "No payment required to inquire."
- "We'll follow up with availability and pricing."
- "Everything is already handled."

---

## OCCASION LANGUAGE

| Occasion | Preferred Language |
|----------|--------------------|
| Bachelorette | "the bride and her crew" / "her last sail before the veil" |
| Birthday | "the birthday girl and her people" / "a day designed for her" |
| Girls trip | "the group" / "your people" / "everyone you'd actually want there" |
| Anniversary | "just the two of you" / "your kind of celebration" |

Never use "gals" or "ladies" or "babes".

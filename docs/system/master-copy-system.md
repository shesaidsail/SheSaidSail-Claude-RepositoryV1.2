# She Said Sail: Master Copy System
Version: 1.0

This is the authoritative copy system for every piece of text on the She Said Sail website. Every future page, section, form, and CTA follows these rules.

---

## 1. BRAND VOICE DEFINITION

The She Said Sail voice is defined by five qualities. Every sentence should satisfy at least three of them.

**Composed:** The writing is calm. It does not rush, persuade, or perform. It creates space for the reader to arrive at her own conclusion.

**Warm:** It sounds like a trusted friend who has excellent taste, not a sales team. It notices people. It remembers why the occasion matters.

**Specific:** It says exactly what it means. No filler. No abstraction. "The golden light at 6pm on Biscayne Bay" is better than "a beautiful setting."

**Elevated:** The language is refined but never pretentious. It does not show off. It does not use long words when short ones are more precise.

**Observant:** It notices the things that matter. The golden light. The sound of the water. The moment after the toast. The way a group exhales when the dock disappears behind them.

---

## 2. SENTENCE RHYTHM

Good copy for She Said Sail is musical. It has pace, variation, and breath.

**Rules:**

- Sentences are short. Mostly 8 to 14 words.
- Vary rhythm with occasional longer sentences for emotional weight. The longer sentence earns its length by saying something that requires space.
- Use fragments deliberately. Not sloppily. "Just the eight of you. The water. The afternoon."
- Never begin a sentence with "We are proud to offer" or any similar corporate opening.
- Never use passive voice for CTAs. Active voice only.
- One idea per sentence.
- Paragraph length: 2 to 3 sentences maximum.
- Read every paragraph aloud. If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.

**Examples of rhythm done well:**

> She did not want a party. She wanted a day she would remember for the right reasons.
> This is what that looks like.

> The boat is beautiful. That is not the point.
> The point is the afternoon, the group, the occasion you came here to honor.

---

## 3. HEADLINE RULES

Headlines lead with feeling, not feature. The reader should see herself in the headline before she reads the subhead.

**Rules:**

- Headlines lead with feeling, not feature.
- Headlines are statements. Not questions.
- Never use superlatives: best, most, #1, unparalleled, premier.
- Max 10 words for hero headlines (H1).
- Max 12 words for section headings (H2, H3).
- Never use a colon in a headline unless the colon creates genuine rhythm.
- Italic headlines in Playfair Display communicate emotional warmth. Use for hero H1 line 2 and for major section headings where warmth is the priority.

**Correct:**

> The day that actually felt like a celebration.
> She said yes. You said Biscayne Bay.
> A few hours that belong entirely to you.

**Incorrect:**

> Miami's Premier Yacht Charter Service
> Book the Best Yacht Experience Now
> Unforgettable Luxury on the Water

---

## 4. SUBHEADLINE RULES

The subheadline expands on the headline. It does not repeat it.

**Rules:**

- One to three sentences.
- Always conversational tone. Never jargon.
- Never end with "...and more."
- Never use "world-class," "unmatched," or "unparalleled."
- The subheadline should add a specific detail the headline could not contain.
- It should confirm: this is for you. This is real. This is what happens.

**Correct:**

> We work with a small number of groups at a time. Your occasion gets real attention from start to finish. Biscayne Bay is the backdrop. Everything else is yours to shape.

**Incorrect:**

> We offer world-class yacht experiences for all types of celebrations. Our professional team is ready to help you create an unforgettable day on the water and more.

---

## 5. CTA COPY

CTAs at She Said Sail are permission-giving. They invite. They do not pressure. The guest decides at her pace.

**Accepted CTA language:**

- Request to Book
- Explore the Experiences
- See What It Looks Like
- Join
- Get in Touch
- Plan Your Occasion
- Tell Us About Your Group

**Never use as CTAs:**

- Book Now
- Submit
- Submit Inquiry
- Click Here
- Learn More
- Get Started
- Sign Up
- Reserve Your Spot
- Claim Your Date

**CTA tone notes:**

The primary CTA is "Request to Book," not "Book Now." The difference is not semantic. It signals to the visitor that submitting a request is the beginning of a conversation, not a transaction. This reduces friction and increases qualified submissions.

Secondary CTAs should feel like natural invitations to continue exploring. "Explore the Experiences" works because it promises discovery, not commitment.

---

## 6. FORM COPY RULES

Forms are the most vulnerable moment in the visitor experience. The copy must feel like a concierge, not a software interface.

**Field labels:**

- Simple nouns or short, natural questions.
- Correct: "Your name" / "Occasion type" / "Group size"
- Incorrect: "Full Legal Name" / "Select Event Category" / "Number of Attendees"

**Placeholder text:**

- Lowercase. Gentle. Conversational.
- Correct: "your email address" / "tell us about the occasion"
- Incorrect: "Enter your email here" / "Please describe your event"

**Required fields:**

- Mark with an asterisk (*).
- Include one small note below the form: "Fields marked * are required."
- Never use red asterisks inline. The goal is low anxiety, not compliance signaling.

**Submit button:**

- "Send My Request" or "Request to Book."
- Never "Submit." Never "Go." Never "Book Now."

**Success message:**

- Warm and personal. Written as if from a real person.
- Correct: "We have your request. A member of our team will be in touch within 24 hours."
- Incorrect: "Form submitted successfully. You will receive a confirmation email."

**Error messages:**

- Helpful, not alarming. Specific, not technical.
- Correct: "Please check your email address." / "Your message looks a little short. Tell us a bit more."
- Incorrect: "Invalid input." / "Error 422: field validation failed."

---

## 7. REASSURANCE LANGUAGE

Some visitors are interested but uncertain. They need permission to take the next step without feeling committed. Use this language naturally, not as a disclaimer block.

**Approved reassurance phrases:**

- "No commitment required. Submitting a request is not a booking."
- "We work with a small number of groups at a time."
- "Every request is reviewed personally."
- "We will be in touch within 24 hours."
- "Your information is private."
- "This is the beginning of a conversation, not a contract."

**Never use:**

- "100% satisfaction guaranteed"
- "Risk-free"
- "No questions asked"
- "We'll never spam you" (sounds defensive; instead: "We treat your inbox the way we treat our guests.")
- "No hidden fees" (raises the question of whether fees are hidden)

---

## 8. TRUST LANGUAGE

She Said Sail builds trust through specificity, not through claims of excellence. The goal is to make the visitor feel she is in capable, caring hands before she ever makes contact.

**Signals that build trust without bragging:**

- Specific occasion types mentioned by name: bachelorette, birthday, girls trip, relationship celebration. Self-identification is trust. When the visitor sees her occasion named, she relaxes.
- Named experiences: Monaco Social, Golden Hour Escape. Specificity signals that real thought went into the offering. It is the opposite of a generic "package."
- Real attribution in testimonials: first name, last initial, occasion type, experience name.
- Price anchor with no apology: "Starting from $10,000." Stated plainly, it qualifies visitors and signals that this is a serious offering.
- Concierge language: "a real person," "within 24 hours," "personally reviewed."

**Never use:**

- "Trusted by thousands"
- "5-star rated" without a named platform and linked review count
- Award or badge language unless the award is specifically named (e.g., "Named Best Charter Experience by Miami New Times, 2024")
- Vague social proof: "our guests love us" / "hundreds of happy clients"

---

## 9. TESTIMONIAL TONE

Testimonials are one of the most powerful trust signals on the site. They must sound like real people, not marketing copy.

**What a good testimonial looks like:**

- Written in first person, natural voice
- Specific: mentions the occasion, the experience, or a real moment from the day
- Emotionally honest: not hyperbolic, not written in marketing language
- Short: 2 to 4 sentences maximum
- Attribution format: First name, last initial. Occasion type. Experience name.

**Good example:**

> "We did the Golden Hour Escape for my sister's bachelorette. I was nervous nothing would live up to the hype. It did. The afternoon felt completely unhurried."
> Camille R. Bachelorette. Golden Hour Escape.

**Bad testimonials (do not publish):**

- Generic: "Best yacht charter in Miami! 5 stars! Highly recommend!"
- Hyperbolic: "The most incredible day of my entire life!"
- Corporate-sounding: "Professional service and excellent value. The team was very organized."
- Marketing-adjacent: "If you want a truly unforgettable luxury experience, look no further."

If a real submitted testimonial uses prohibited language, paraphrase with the guest's approval before publishing.

---

## 10. PRICING LANGUAGE

The price is not an apology. It is a signal. State it with the same calm confidence as everything else on the site.

**Rules:**

- Always: "Starting from $10,000"
- Never: "Affordable luxury" or "Value packages available"
- Never apologize for the price. State it plainly and let it stand.
- Never use "investment" as a substitute for "price." It sounds like a sales script.
- Context sentence: "Starting from $10,000, with pricing based on group size, experience, and date."
- If a range is needed: "Starting from $10,000. Most groups are between $10,000 and $18,000."

**Price placement:**

- Price appears in the experiences section, in the request to book page, and wherever a visitor might ask "but how much is this?"
- It does not need to appear in the hero. Let the experience establish its value first.

## 11. PHRASES TO NEVER USE (expanded)

**Corporate clichés:**

world-class, unparalleled, premier, top-tier, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, seamless experience, next level, game-changing, turnkey, best-in-class, industry-leading

**Fake urgency:**

limited availability (unless literally verified), book before it is gone, selling fast, last spots remaining, don't miss out, act now

**Hype language:**

amazing, awesome, incredible, epic, insane, mind-blowing, unforgettable, life-changing, jaw-dropping, breathtaking (as a cliché, not as a specific observation)

**Weak filler words:**

really, very, truly, absolutely, definitely, of course, simply, just (as emphasis), so (as emphasis)

**Nautical clichés used as wordplay:**

set sail, ahoy, all aboard, smooth sailing, bon voyage, anchors aweigh, cast off, sea you there, make waves (as a pun)

**Forbidden per brand guidelines:**

VIP, best yacht, party boat, luxury rental, submit inquiry, book now, exclusive deal, once in a lifetime, premium package, amazing, awesome, unforgettable, elite, baller, exclusive access, epic, next-level

## 12. PAGE-BY-PAGE COPY NOTES

**Homepage:**

Lead with feeling and occasion, not with the product. The visitor should recognize her own celebration before she learns anything about the boat. "Curated Yacht Experiences" is acceptable as a category descriptor but should not be a headline. The hero copy positions the occasion and the feeling. The boat is the setting, not the subject.

**Experiences Page:**

Each experience has a distinct personality. The Monaco Social feels like an evening in a European port city. The Golden Hour Escape feels like the day slowing down at exactly the right moment. Let each experience speak in its own register. Copy is specific and editorial, not list-formatted or spec-driven.

**Request to Book Page:**

The visitor is already interested. She has made it to the form. The copy's job is to reassure, not to resell. Conversational, warm, concierge-led. The form should feel like handing your request to a real person. Reassurance language appears above and around the form, not in a disclaimer below it.

**Thank You Page:**

The visitor has already converted. The copy's job is warmth, confirmation, and a clear statement of next steps. Do not upsell on the thank you page. Do not redirect to the experiences page. Let her sit with the decision she just made.

**Blog or Editorial Pages (if added):**

Write in the same voice as the rest of the site. Informative but never listicle-formatted. No numbered "top 10" headlines. Treat editorial content as a continuation of the brand's point of view.

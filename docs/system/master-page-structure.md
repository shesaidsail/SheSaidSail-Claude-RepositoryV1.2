# She Said Sail: Master Page Structure System
Version: 1.0

This is the standard for how every page on the She Said Sail website is structured. Section-by-section pacing guides the visitor through an emotional experience, not just an information sequence. Every future page follows this structure.

---

## 1. STRUCTURE PHILOSOPHY

The page is a conversation, not a catalog.

A catalog presents everything at once and lets the visitor sort through it. A conversation moves deliberately. It earns each step before asking for the next one.

Every She Said Sail page moves the visitor through four emotional states, in this order:

1. **Recognition:** This is for me.
2. **Aspiration:** I want this.
3. **Trust:** I believe this is real and worth it.
4. **Action:** I am ready to reach out.

Sections are placed in that order. Content that creates recognition comes first. Content that requires trust comes after the experience has been established. The CTA comes last, when it has been earned.

A visitor who feels rushed to the CTA will leave. A visitor who moves through this sequence at her own pace will convert.

---

## 2. UNIVERSAL PAGE SECTIONS

Every page on the site includes these sections, in this standard order:

| Position | Section | Required |
|----------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Sticky navigation | Always |
| 2 | Page hero | Always |
| 3 | Trust or social proof placement | Before or after primary content, within first 3 sections for conversion pages |
| 4 | Primary content section(s) | Always |
| 5 | CTA section | Before footer, always |
| 6 | Email capture | Optional, for non-conversion pages only |
| 7 | Global footer | Always |

---

## 3. HERO STRUCTURE

The hero is the first thing the visitor sees. It must do three things immediately: establish the brand register, position the occasion, and invite the visitor to stay.

**Every hero section includes:**

- Full-width photography or video (no sidebars, no containers)
- Navigation overlay: sticky, transparent on initial load, transitions to solid on scroll
- Eyebrow label (optional): 10px uppercase, tracked, cream, no more than 4 words
- Headline H1 line 1: cream, Cormorant Garamond, serif weight
- Headline H1 line 2: gold italic, Playfair Display, the emotional line
- Subheadline (optional): 17px Inter light, cream at 0.85 opacity, maximum 2 sentences
- Occasion pills (optional): homepage and experiences page only, not on conversion pages
- Primary CTA button: white or outlined, centered below subheadline
- Hero height: 88vh desktop, 92vh mobile

**Hero overlay:**

The photography must be visible. Overlay opacity must not exceed 0.40. On images that are naturally dark or atmospheric (golden hour, evening), the overlay may be reduced to 0.20 to 0.28. The goal is text legibility with full photographic warmth behind it.

**Hero photography direction:**

- Lifestyle over product. People, golden light, water, and moments over empty boats.
- Warm color temperature. No cold blue tones.
- Never: stock photography that reads as generic travel or charter imagery.
- Always: photography where the occasion feels present even if no faces are shown.

## 4. SOCIAL PROOF PLACEMENT

Rule: social proof appears within the first 3 sections of any page that asks for a $10,000 commitment.

**Page-specific placement:**

- Homepage: between the experience cards section and the value proposition section. The visitor has seen what is offered; social proof validates it before the next ask.
- Experiences page: below the individual experience cards. After specifics have been presented, validation follows.
- Request to Book page: above the form, in the concierge reassurance block. The visitor is about to submit a request. She needs to feel that real people are on the other side.
- Thank you page: not needed. The visitor has already converted.

**What counts as social proof:**

- Attributed testimonials with occasion type and experience name
- A named statistic ("Over 200 occasions hosted on Biscayne Bay")
- Press mentions with outlet name and date
- A visual strip of guest photos (with consent) alongside a short quote

**What does not count as social proof:**

- Star ratings without a named platform
- "Trusted by hundreds of guests" without attribution
- Generic positive language without specificity

---

## 5. TRUST SIGNAL PLACEMENT

Trust signals are woven throughout the page, not concentrated in a single "why us" section. A dedicated "why choose She Said Sail" section reads as defensive. Distributed trust signals read as confident.

**Standard sequence for conversion pages:**

1. Hero: brand positioning and visual quality. The photography itself is a trust signal.
2. Experience cards: specificity. Named, detailed experiences signal that real thought went into the offering.
3. Social proof strip: external validation from real guests with real attributions.
4. Concierge or "how it works" section: internal validation. Real people. Real process.
5. Price anchor: qualification signal. Stating the starting price plainly builds trust with the right visitor and gracefully filters out mismatched ones.
6. Final CTA: arrives after trust has been established. It is a conclusion, not a demand.

---

## 6. CTA HIERARCHY

Every page has exactly one primary CTA. Supporting CTAs exist to serve visitors who are not yet ready for the primary action.

**Hierarchy:**

| Level | CTA Copy | Destination | Visual Weight |
|-------|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Primary | Request to Book | /request-to-book/ | Filled button, gold or white |
| Secondary | Explore the Experiences | /experiences/ | Outlined button or text link |
| Supporting | Email capture | Stays on page | Inline, minimal |

**Rules:**

- Never place two CTAs with equal visual weight on the same screen.
- The primary CTA must be visually dominant. The secondary CTA must be clearly subordinate.
- On mobile, display one CTA per visible screen area.
- The final CTA section before the footer is always the primary.

---

## 7. SECTION RHYTHM PATTERN

Every content section follows a 3-beat emotional arc. The structure is consistent even when the visual layout varies.

**Standard 3-beat rhythm:**

- Beat 1: Eyebrow label + heading. Sets the context. Tells the visitor where she is and what this section is about.
- Beat 2: Subtext or body copy. Adds meaning, emotion, or specific detail. The reason to care.
- Beat 3: CTA or supporting content. Invites action or continues the journey to the next section.

**Extended rhythm for richer sections:**

Experience cards, testimonial strips, and "how it works" sections may extend this pattern. The emotional arc stays the same: establish, deepen, invite. The pacing slows down in the middle sections of the page to create space before the CTA.

**Eyebrow label format:**

- All caps, 10px, tracked
- Maximum 4 words
- Never a sentence. Never punctuated.
- Examples: CURATED EXPERIENCES / BISCAYNE BAY / FOR YOUR OCCASION / WHAT GUESTS SAY

---

## 8. SECTION BACKGROUND SEQUENCE

Background color alternates to create visual rhythm and breathing room between sections. Never two sections of the same dark background adjacent to each other.

**Background palette:**

| Background | Use Case |
|-----------|---------|
| White (#FFFFFF) | Default content sections, experience cards |
| Warm cream (#FAF8F3 or #F5F0E8) | Email capture, reassurance sections, softer trust moments |
| Navy (#1A2332) | Major CTAs, social proof strip, final CTA banner |
| Full-width image | Visual breathing moments between dense content |

**Sequencing rules:**

- Never two navy sections back to back. Always a white or cream section between them.
- Never two text-heavy white sections back to back without a full-width image between them.
- The final section before the footer is almost always navy: it signals conclusion and invites action.

---

## 9. MOBILE STACKING

All multi-column desktop layouts collapse to single column below 767px. The order on mobile must maintain the emotional logic of the section.

**Rules:**

- CTA always appears below the copy that motivates it. Never above.
- On experience cards: headline, image, description, then CTA. In that order.
- Social proof cards: most powerful or specific quote appears first on mobile.
- Two-column layouts with image left and text right: text stacks above image on mobile when the text leads the emotional sequence. Image stacks above text when the image leads.
- Navigation: collapses to hamburger. Priority links are: Experiences, Request to Book, Contact.
- Form fields: full width on mobile. No side-by-side fields below 767px.

---

## 10. EMOTIONAL PACING

The homepage does not rush to the CTA. Six to eight sections between the hero and the final CTA banner is correct. Each section earns the right to the next section.

**Rules:**

- Do not place the primary CTA in the hero on the homepage. The visitor needs context first.
- On the Request to Book page, the CTA (the form) can appear earlier. The visitor arrived with intent.
- On the experiences page, individual experience CTAs point to the request to book page, not to an external booking engine.
- The final navy CTA section should feel like a natural conclusion: "We have shown you what we offer. You have seen what guests say. Now here is how to begin."

**Pacing test:**

Read the page in order. At each section, ask: has this visitor been given enough reason to take the next step? If the answer is no, the section before the CTA has not done its job. Add specificity, warmth, or social proof before moving forward.

---

## 11. IMAGE PACING

Full-width images are visual breathing moments. They give the eye a rest and let the emotional content of the previous section settle.

**Rules:**

- Place a full-width image between dense content sections.
- Never two text-heavy sections side by side without a visual break.
- The hero is always full-width. No exceptions.
- Interior full-width images should be photographically consistent with the hero: warm light, lifestyle over product, Biscayne Bay.
- Full-width images do not need text overlays. They can exist purely as visual pauses.
- Aspect ratio: full-width images are 16:9 or 3:1 (cinematic) on desktop. 4:3 on mobile.

---

## 12. FOOTER STRUCTURE AND PLACEMENT

The footer is always the last element on every page. It provides navigation, contact information, and brand closure. It does not repeat the final CTA.

**Footer contains:**

- Logo (links back to homepage)
- Tagline (one line, brand voice, beneath the logo)
- Primary navigation links: Experiences, Request to Book, About, Contact
- Contact information: phone number (linked), email address
- Social links: Instagram, optionally Pinterest
- Copyright line: "She Said Sail. Miami, FL."
- Legal links if required: Privacy Policy, Terms

**Footer rules:**

- No CTAs in the footer. The footer is not a conversion section.
- The logo link is the only navigational anchor in the footer logo area.
- Footer background: navy or deep charcoal. Never white. It signals the end of the page.
- Footer typography: cream on navy. Small, clean, generous line spacing.
- Do not repeat pricing, testimonials, or experience descriptions in the footer.

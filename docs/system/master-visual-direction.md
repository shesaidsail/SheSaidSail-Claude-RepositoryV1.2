# She Said Sail: Master Visual Direction
Version: 1.0
Last Updated: 2026-05-18

---

## 1. VISUAL PHILOSOPHY

The site communicates one specific feeling: "I wish I was there." Not staged. Not commercial. Genuinely warm, human, and golden. Every photograph, every frame of video, and every design composition should earn its place by answering the question: does this make someone feel something? The visual identity of She Said Sail is built on the premise that the most luxurious thing a brand can offer is authenticity. Not perfection. Not spectacle. Real women, real light, real moments on the water. The role of photography and visual direction is to make the product feel inevitable, not aspirational in an unattainable way. The woman viewing the site should see herself in it.

---

## 2. PHOTOGRAPHY STANDARDS

### What to Always Capture

**Light:**
- Natural golden light is the standard. Morning light (7-10am) and late afternoon light (3-6pm) are preferred shooting windows.
- Warm color temperature. The ocean should appear green-gold or blue-green, never cold or grey.
- Soft, directional light on faces. Avoid harsh midday overhead light.
- Reflections on water: light catching the surface is a recurring visual motif.

**The Water:**
- Calm or gently rippled. Never stormy or foreboding.
- Warm-colored: the light should make the water feel inviting, not clinical.
- The water as context and mood, not just background.

**The Women:**
- In moments of ease: laughing mid-sentence, eyes closed resting, in genuine conversation, looking at the horizon.
- Groups that look genuinely comfortable with each other. The chemistry should read as real.
- Never directed in a way that looks directed. The camera should feel like it found the moment, not staged it.
- Diversity in age (25-50 range), background, and style. This experience is for real women, not a single archetype.
- Women who look like they belong somewhere beautiful.

**The Details:**
- Glasses of champagne or wine in relaxed hands, not raised for a cheers
- Fabric catching the breeze
- Flowers in casual arrangements
- Hands: relaxed, holding something, resting on the railing
- The water surface close up: light patterns, ripple texture
- Light on skin: golden hour glow is a priority
- The yacht itself as setting, not product

**The Feeling:**
- Having nowhere to be
- Being fully present
- The best version of an ordinary afternoon
- The kind of ease that only comes when everything is taken care of

### What to Never Photograph

- Formal, stiff group poses where everyone faces the camera at the same angle
- Men as primary subjects. She Said Sail is a women-led brand. Male crew may appear incidentally but are never the visual subject.
- Synchronized party poses: everyone raising their glasses in unison, jumping, forced expressions
- Dark or moody editing that makes the water or sky feel cold or melancholy
- Cold blue/grey tones in color grading
- Logos, brand names, or commercial products visible in frame
- Generic stock photography of empty yachts without people
- Overly revealing clothing that shifts the tone from luxury to nightlife
- Drunk or sloppy energy. The mood is ease, not excess.

## 3. EDITING STYLE

### Color Grade

| Property | Direction | Hard Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Color temperature | Slightly elevated (warmer) | Never cooler than neutral |
| Saturation | Slightly lifted | Never oversaturated. Skin tones must remain natural. |
| Shadows | Lifted. Open, airy shadows. | Never crushed blacks. |
| Highlights | Protected. Gentle rolloff. | Water and sky must not blow out. |
| Skin tones | True-to-life, warm | Never orange. Never washed out or grey. |
| Grain | Fine grain acceptable | No heavy grain. No film emulation that obscures detail. |

### Reference Aesthetic

The editing target is: warm editorial. Think Vogue Spa Issue, not Instagram Travel Blogger. The images should look expensive because they are restrained, not because they are heavily processed.

Presets to avoid entirely:
- Faded/matte looks (lifted blacks without intention)
- Cool-toned presets
- High-contrast dramatic edits
- Desaturated skin tones
- Heavy vignettes

### Skin Tone Priority

Skin tone accuracy is non-negotiable. Every person photographed should look like themselves, with warmth and glow, but never artificially altered. Run a final check on skin tones before approving any image for the site.

## 4. COMPOSITION STANDARDS

### Hero Images

- Rule of thirds: primary subject (person or group) in the left or right third
- The remaining two-thirds filled by water, horizon, or open sky
- Always provide enough visual weight in the center of the frame that the hero section heading does not compete with a busy area
- The overlay will sit at 0.36 opacity. Compose with that in mind: images with lower-contrast centers work best.
- Horizon line: ideally slightly above the vertical center, giving the water more visual weight

### Card and Feature Images

- Portrait orientation (4:5) is acceptable for experience cards
- Square crops (1:1) for social media derivatives
- Always leave breathing room around subjects. No tight crops against the frame edge.
- People should never be cropped at the neck, wrists, or ankle. Crop above or below joints.

### Desktop Hero Crops

- 16:9 minimum for desktop hero sections
- The image must carry emotional weight at the horizontal center
- Avoid compositions where the most important element is at the far edge (it will be off-screen on some viewports)

### Social Media

- Square 1:1 crops for all Instagram content
- Portrait 4:5 for Reels covers and Stories covers
- Always test crops at actual output dimensions before posting

## 5. WHAT IMAGES COMMUNICATE

Each image selected for the site should communicate at least one of the following. If an image cannot be matched to any of these, it does not belong on the site.

| Message | Visual Cues That Carry It |
|---|---|
| "This feels like my people." | Genuine connection between subjects, real expressions, comfortable body language |
| "I want to be in this moment." | Environmental beauty, golden light, calm water, a feeling of presence |
| "This is effortless and taken care of." | Relaxed posture, unhurried energy, beautiful surroundings without visible effort |
| "This is the kind of day I deserve." | Elevated details: quality fabrics, beautiful glassware, gorgeous light, space and time |
| "This is more elevated than I expected." | Unexpected beauty in the composition, quality of light, refinement in the details |

When reviewing photography for approval, ask: which of these five messages does this image deliver? If the answer is none, the image does not pass.

## 6. IMAGERY TO AVOID

The following categories of imagery actively damage the She Said Sail brand. These are not aesthetic preferences. They are brand protection rules.

| Category | Why It Harms the Brand |
|---|---|
| Nightclub energy: neon, aggressive expressions, raised bottles in unison | Signals party-boat energy, repels the luxury customer |
| Generic luxury clichés: champagne towers, stiff poses on deck | Looks like every other yacht rental company |
| Overly formal or corporate event photography | Conflicts with the warm, human brand positioning |
| Cold, grey, or melancholy editorial photography | Contradicts the warmth and ease the brand is built on |
| Stock yacht photography without people | Sells a product, not an experience |
| Synchronized pose photography | Signals inauthenticity. Real moments do not look choreographed. |
| Images where women look uncomfortable, performative, or self-conscious | The brand is built on ease. Visible performance breaks the illusion. |

## 7. VIDEO DIRECTION

Video on the She Said Sail website is optional but powerful when executed correctly. The following standards govern any video content used on the site or embedded in pages.

### Capture Standards

- Slow motion: accepted and preferred for water sequences, fabric in wind, any movement that benefits from elongation
- Golden hour: same preference as photography. Avoid shooting primary content in midday light.
- Handheld with very gentle motion is acceptable. Aggressive handheld or jerky motion is not.
- No action camera footage (GoPro distortion aesthetic conflicts with the brand)

### Music and Sound

- Warm, subtle, non-intrusive background music only
- No hip-hop, EDM, or tracks with aggressive tempo or heavy bass
- No voiceover. The visuals carry the emotion.
- Preferred: ambient piano, quiet acoustic, warm instrumental. Nothing that could be described as a "hype track."

### Length Standards

| Context | Target Length |
|---|---|
| Homepage background video (looping) | 15-30 seconds |
| Social media short-form video | 15-30 seconds |
| Feature / brand video | 60-90 seconds maximum |
| Testimonial video | 60-90 seconds per person |

### Autoplay Rules

- Background videos may autoplay only if: muted, looping, no audio, and a pause control is available
- Never autoplay a video with sound
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: if the user has this preference set, the background video should not autoplay. Show a static image fallback.

### Thumbnail and Poster Frames

- Every video must have a high-quality poster frame that matches the visual direction standards
- Poster frames are what the user sees during load and before play. They must be as carefully selected as photography.
